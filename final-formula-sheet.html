Unit 4 — Conic Sections Ch 10.4–10.5

Eccentricity

$$ e = \frac{PF}{PD} = \frac{\text{dist. to focus}}{\text{dist. to directrix}} $$
\(e = 0\)Circle
\(0 < e < 1\)Ellipse
\(e = 1\)Parabola
\(e > 1\)Hyperbola
Ellipse & hyperbola: \(e = c/a\). Use the range as a sanity check.

Parabola · \(PF = PD\)

EquationOpensFocusDirectrix
\(y^2 = 4ax\)Right\((a,0)\)\(x=-a\)
\(y^2 = -4ax\)Left\((-a,0)\)\(x=a\)
\(x^2 = 4ay\)Up\((0,a)\)\(y=-a\)
\(x^2 = -4ay\)Down\((0,-a)\)\(y=a\)
Vertex \((0,0)\) · latus rectum \(= 4a\) · \(e = 1\)
Shifted to vertex \((h, k)\)
$$ (x-h)^2 = 4a(y-k) $$
Focus \((h, k+a)\), directrix \(y = k - a\)
Match the number to \(4a\): \(y^2 = 12x \Rightarrow a = 3\).

Ellipse · \(PF_1 + PF_2 = 2a\)

$$ \frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1, \qquad a > b $$
Vertices / co-vertices\((\pm a, 0)\) / \((0, \pm b)\)
Major / minor axis\(2a\) / \(2b\)
Focal distance\(c^2 = a^2 - b^2\)
Foci\((\pm c, 0) = (\pm ae, 0)\)
Eccentricity\(e = \frac{c}{a} = \sqrt{1 - \frac{b^2}{a^2}}\)
Directrices\(x = \pm \frac{a}{e}\)
Latus rectum\(\frac{2b^2}{a}\)
Larger denominator is \(a^2\) — its variable is the major-axis direction.

Hyperbola · \(|PF_1 - PF_2| = 2a\)

$$ \frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1 $$
Vertices\((\pm a, 0)\)
Transverse / conjugate\(2a\) / \(2b\)
Focal distance\(c^2 = a^2 + b^2\)
Foci\((\pm c, 0) = (\pm ae, 0)\)
Eccentricity\(e = \frac{c}{a} = \frac{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}{a}\)
Directrices\(x = \pm \frac{a}{e}\)
Asymptotes\(y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x\)
Positive term is \(a^2\) (size doesn't matter). Vertical: \(\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1\), asymptotes \(y = \pm\frac{a}{b}x\).
Asymptotes: set the RHS to 0 and solve for \(y\).

Translated Conics & Identifying

Centre \((h, k)\) — shift every point
$$ \frac{(x-h)^2}{a^2} \pm \frac{(y-k)^2}{b^2} = 1 $$
Vertices \((h \pm a, k)\), foci \((h \pm c, k)\), asymptotes \(y - k = \pm\frac{b}{a}(x - h)\)
\(Ax^2 + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0\)
\(A = C\)Circle
\(A, C\) same signEllipse
\(A, C\) opposite signsHyperbola
\(A = 0\) or \(C = 0\)Parabola
Completing the square: what you add inside \(k(\;\cdot\;)\) adds \(k\times\) that to the RHS.

Rotation of Axes

$$ Ax^2 + Bxy + Cy^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0 $$
\(B^2 - 4AC < 0\)Ellipse
\(B^2 - 4AC = 0\)Parabola
\(B^2 - 4AC > 0\)Hyperbola
$$ \cot 2\theta = \frac{A - C}{B} \qquad (A = C \Rightarrow \theta = 45^\circ) $$
$$ x = x'\cos\theta - y'\sin\theta, \quad y = x'\sin\theta + y'\cos\theta $$
$$ A' = A\cos^2\theta + B\sin\theta\cos\theta + C\sin^2\theta $$
$$ C' = A\sin^2\theta - B\sin\theta\cos\theta + C\cos^2\theta $$
$$ D' = D\cos\theta + E\sin\theta $$
$$ E' = -D\sin\theta + E\cos\theta $$
$$ \cos\theta = \sqrt{\tfrac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2}}, \quad \sin\theta = \sqrt{\tfrac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{2}} $$
Check: \(A' + C' = A + C\), and \(B^2 - 4AC\) is unchanged.
Discriminant \(= 0\) but no \(Y\) term after rotating (e.g. \((X-1)^2 = 1\)) → degenerate: a pair of parallel lines, no vertex.
Unit 5 — Polar, Cylindrical & Spherical Ch 11.8, 14.6

Polar \((r, \theta)\)

$$ x = r\cos\theta, \qquad y = r\sin\theta $$
$$ r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}, \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{y}{x} $$
$$ x^2 + y^2 = r^2 $$
\(\theta\) from reference angle \(\alpha = \tan^{-1}|y/x|\)
QI: \(\alpha\)QII: \(\pi - \alpha\)QIII: \(\pi + \alpha\)QIV: \(2\pi - \alpha\)
Never trust \(\tan^{-1}(y/x)\) alone — sketch the quadrant.

Polar Curves

PolarCartesianCurve
\(r = a\)\(x^2 + y^2 = a^2\)Circle at origin
\(\theta = \alpha\)\(y = x\tan\alpha\)Line through origin
\(r = 2a\cos\theta\)\(x^2 + y^2 = 2ax\)Circle, centre \((a, 0)\)
\(r = 2a\sin\theta\)\(x^2 + y^2 = 2ay\)Circle, centre \((0, a)\)
\(r = a(1 + \cos\theta)\)—Cardioid
\(r = 2a\cos\theta\): \(-\tfrac{\pi}{2} \le \theta \le \tfrac{\pi}{2}\). \(r = 2a\sin\theta\): \(0 \le \theta \le \pi\). Using \(0\)–\(2\pi\) doubles the area.

The Jacobian

$$ J = \frac{\partial(x,y)}{\partial(u,v)} = \begin{vmatrix} x_u & x_v \\ y_u & y_v \end{vmatrix} $$
$$ dx\,dy = |J|\,du\,dv $$
Polar
$$ J = \begin{vmatrix} \cos\theta & -r\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & r\cos\theta \end{vmatrix} = r $$
Example: \(x = uv,\ y = u/v\)
$$ J = -\frac{2u}{v} \;\Rightarrow\; dx\,dy = \frac{2u}{v}\,du\,dv $$
Jacobian = how much a tiny area is stretched. Always take \(|J|\).

Double Integrals in Polar

$$ \iint_R f\,dA = \int_\alpha^\beta\!\!\int_{r_1}^{r_2} f(r\cos\theta, r\sin\theta)\,r\,dr\,d\theta $$
$$ \text{Area} = \iint r\,dr\,d\theta = \frac12\int_\alpha^\beta r(\theta)^2\,d\theta $$
Trig you'll need
$$ \cos^2\theta = \tfrac{1 + \cos 2\theta}{2}, \quad \sin^2\theta = \tfrac{1 - \cos 2\theta}{2} $$
$$ \int_0^{2\pi}\!\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = \pi, \quad \int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\!\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} $$
$$ \int_0^{\pi}\sin\phi\,d\phi = 2, \quad \int_0^{\pi/2}\sin\phi\,d\phi = 1 $$

Cylindrical \((r, \theta, z)\)

$$ x = r\cos\theta, \quad y = r\sin\theta, \quad z = z $$
$$ r = \sqrt{x^2+y^2} \;\;(\text{distance from } z\text{-axis}) $$
$$ dV = r\,dz\,dr\,d\theta $$
\(r = a\)Cylinder\(z = r^2\)Paraboloid
\(z = r\)Cone\(r^2 + z^2 = a^2\)Sphere
\(z\) limits first (bottom surface → top surface), then the polar limits of the shadow.

Spherical \((\rho, \theta, \phi)\)

$$ x = \rho\sin\phi\cos\theta, \quad y = \rho\sin\phi\sin\theta $$
$$ z = \rho\cos\phi $$
$$ \rho = \sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}, \quad \phi = \cos^{-1}\frac{z}{\rho} $$
$$ r = \rho\sin\phi, \qquad z = \rho\cos\phi $$
$$ dV = \rho^2\sin\phi\;d\rho\,d\phi\,d\theta $$
\(\rho \ge 0\),  \(0 \le \theta \le 2\pi\),  \(0 \le \phi \le \pi\) (down from \(+z\))
\(\rho = a\)Sphere\(\phi = \alpha\)Cone
\(\phi = \tfrac{\pi}{2}\)\(xy\)-plane\(\theta = \alpha\)Half-plane
Above the \(xy\)-plane → \(0 \le \phi \le \tfrac{\pi}{2}\). \(\phi\) never exceeds \(\pi\).

Unit 6 — Gradient, Divergence & Curl Ch 13.6, 15.1–15.4

Fields & the Gradient

Scalar field\(f:\mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}\) — one number per point
Vector field\(\mathbf{F} = P\mathbf{i} + Q\mathbf{j} + R\mathbf{k}\)
Contour\(f(x,y) = c\)
$$ \nabla = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathbf{i} + \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\mathbf{j} + \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\mathbf{k} $$
$$ \nabla f = \langle f_x,\ f_y,\ f_z\rangle $$
\(\nabla f\) points to steepest increase, has length = that max rate, and is ⟂ to contours.
Differentiate first, substitute the point second.

Directional Derivative

$$ D_{\mathbf{u}}f = \nabla f \cdot \mathbf{u}, \qquad \mathbf{u} = \frac{\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|} $$
$$ D_{\mathbf{u}}f = |\nabla f|\cos\theta $$
Max rate\(|\nabla f|\), along \(\nabla f\)
Min rate\(-|\nabla f|\), along \(-\nabla f\)
Zero⟂ \(\nabla f\) (along contour)
Normalise \(\mathbf{v}\)! From \(P\) to \(Q\): \(\mathbf{v} = \overrightarrow{PQ}\).

Normals, Tangent Planes, Lagrange

Surface \(F(x,y,z) = C\)
$$ \mathbf{n} = \nabla F(x_0, y_0, z_0) $$
$$ F_x(x - x_0) + F_y(y - y_0) + F_z(z - z_0) = 0 $$
Surface \(z = f(x,y)\)
$$ z - z_0 = f_x(x - x_0) + f_y(y - y_0) $$
Lagrange: optimise \(f\) subject to \(g = k\)
$$ \nabla f = \lambda\,\nabla g, \qquad g(x,y) = k $$

Divergence & Curl

$$ \nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = P_x + Q_y + R_z \quad(\text{scalar}) $$
$$ \nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ \partial_x & \partial_y & \partial_z \\ P & Q & R \end{vmatrix} \quad(\text{vector}) $$
$$ = (R_y - Q_z)\,\mathbf{i} + (P_z - R_x)\,\mathbf{j} + (Q_x - P_y)\,\mathbf{k} $$
\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} > 0\) / \(< 0\)Source (expanding) / sink
\(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = 0\)Incompressible
\(\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}\)Irrotational
$$ \nabla\times(\nabla f) = \mathbf{0}, \qquad \nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{F}) = 0 $$

Conservative Fields

$$ \mathbf{F} = \nabla\phi \iff P = \phi_x,\ Q = \phi_y,\ R = \phi_z $$
2D test\(P_y = Q_x\)
3D test\(\nabla\times\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}\)
Finding φ
  • \(\phi = \int P\,dx + g(y,z)\)
  • \(\phi_y = Q\) → solve for \(g\); then \(\phi_z = R\)
  • Add \(+\,C\); check \(\nabla\phi = \mathbf{F}\)
$$ \int_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \phi(B) - \phi(A), \qquad \oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0 $$
Conservative ⟺ potential exists ⟹ path independent.

Line Integrals

Scalar field
$$ \int_C f\,ds = \int_a^b f\big(x(t), y(t)\big)\sqrt{x'^2 + y'^2}\,dt $$
$$ y = g(x):\quad ds = \sqrt{1 + g'(x)^2}\,dx $$
Vector field (work)
$$ \int_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \int_a^b \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t))\cdot\mathbf{r}'(t)\,dt $$
$$ = \int_C P\,dx + Q\,dy\;(+\,R\,dz) $$
Straight line \(A \to B\): \(\mathbf{r}(t) = A + t(B - A)\), \(0 \le t \le 1\)
Check conservative first — it can save the whole integral.

Green's Theorem

$$ \oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_R \left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right) dA $$
\(C\) closed, counterclockwise, bounding \(R\).
Constant integrand \(k\): answer \(= k \times \text{area}(R)\).
It's \(Q_x - P_y\) (not the reverse). Clockwise flips the sign.
Unit 7 — Flux, Surface Integrals & the Big Theorems Ch 15.4–15.8

The Big Three

Green's — flat region R, edge C (counterclockwise)
$$ \oint_C P\,dx + Q\,dy = \iint_R (Q_x - P_y)\,dA $$
Stokes' — curved surface σ, edge C
$$ \oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_\sigma (\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS $$
Divergence (Gauss) — closed surface S around solid G
$$ \oiint_S \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS = \iiint_G \nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}\,dV $$
Same idea each time: add up a derivative inside = look only at the edge. Curl ↔ circulation (around); divergence ↔ flux (out through).

Surface Integrals · \(z = g(x,y)\)

$$ dS = \sqrt{g_x^2 + g_y^2 + 1}\;dA $$
$$ \iint_\sigma f\,dS = $$
$$ \iint_R f\big(x, y, g(x,y)\big)\sqrt{g_x^2 + g_y^2 + 1}\,dA $$
$$ \text{Surface area} = \iint_R \sqrt{g_x^2 + g_y^2 + 1}\;dA $$
Plane \(ax + by + cz = d\)
$$ dS = \frac{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2 + c^2}}{|c|}\,dA \quad(\text{a constant}) $$
\(R\) = the shadow on the \(xy\)-plane (set \(z = 0\) for a plane in the first octant → triangle).
Substitute \(z = g(x,y)\) into \(f\) before integrating.

Flux Through a Surface

$$ \text{Flux} = \iint_\sigma \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{n}\,dS $$
\(z = g(x,y)\), oriented up · \(\mathbf{F} = \langle P, Q, R\rangle\)
$$ \iint_R \big(-P\,g_x - Q\,g_y + R\big)\,dA $$
Oriented down: flip the sign. Disc-shaped shadow → polar.
Flux \(> 0\)Net flow out / along \(\mathbf{n}\)
Flux \(< 0\)Net flow in / against \(\mathbf{n}\)
For \(\mathbf{F} = x\mathbf{i} - y\mathbf{j} + z\mathbf{k}\) on \(z = k - x^2 - y^2\): integrand \(x^2 - 3y^2 + k\), flux \(= \tfrac{\pi k^2}{2}\).

Divergence Theorem — How To

  • Surface must be closed (dome + base, cylinder + top + bottom, box)
  • Compute \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = P_x + Q_y + R_z\)
  • Pick coordinates for the solid; include the Jacobian
SolidLimits
Upper half-ball, radius \(a\)\(\rho\!:0\to a,\ \phi\!:0\to\tfrac{\pi}{2},\ \theta\!:0\to 2\pi\)
Under \(z = k - r^2\), above \(z = 0\)\(z\!:0\to k - r^2,\ r\!:0\to\sqrt{k}\)
Cylinder \(r \le a\), \(z_1 \le z \le z_2\)\(z\!:z_1\to z_2,\ r\!:0\to a\)
Ice-cream cone \(z \ge r\), \(\rho \le a\)\(\phi\!:0\to\tfrac{\pi}{4},\ \rho\!:0\to a\)
Constant divergence \(k\) → flux \(= k \times \text{volume}\). \(x^2+y^2+z^2 = \rho^2\).

Stokes' Theorem — How To

  • Compute \(\nabla\times\mathbf{F}\)
  • Any surface with the same edge \(C\) works — pick the flattest (often a disc, \(\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{k}\))
  • Then \((\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot\mathbf{k}\) = the \(\mathbf{k}\)-component of the curl
$$ \text{Flat in the } xy\text{-plane:} $$
$$ \oint_C \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_R (Q_x - P_y)\,dA $$
That's just Green's theorem — Stokes' is Green's for curved surfaces.
Orientation: counterclockwise edge seen from above ↔ upward \(\mathbf{n}\) (right-hand rule).

Shortcuts & Quick Values

$$ \int_0^{2\pi}\!\cos\theta\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\sin\theta\,d\theta = 0 $$
$$ \int_0^{2\pi}\!\cos^2\theta\,d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi}\!\sin^2\theta\,d\theta = \pi $$
$$ \int_0^{\pi/2}\!\sin\phi\,d\phi = 1 $$
$$ \int_0^{\pi/4}\!\sin\phi\,d\phi = 1 - \tfrac{\sqrt2}{2} $$
  • Integrand odd in \(x\) (or \(y\)) over a centred disc → 0
  • Green's with constant \(Q_x - P_y = k\) → \(k \times\) area
  • Constant limits → integral splits into a product
  • Hemisphere radius \(a\): volume \(\tfrac23\pi a^3\); \(\iiint\rho^2\,dV = \tfrac{2\pi a^5}{5}\)

Final Checklist

  • Ellipse subtracts for \(c\); hyperbola adds
  • Hyperbola: \(a^2\) is under the positive term
  • After completing the square, shift by \((h, k)\)
  • Axis length is \(2a\), not \(a\)
  • Never drop the Jacobian: \(r\) or \(\rho^2\sin\phi\)
  • Sketch the point before choosing \(\theta\)
  • \(\phi \in [0, \pi]\); off-centre circles need \(\theta \in [-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2}]\)
  • \(r\) = distance from \(z\)-axis; \(\rho\) = distance from origin
  • Check eccentricity range and known volumes
  • Directional derivative needs a unit vector
  • Divergence is a scalar; curl is a vector
  • Find \(\phi\) via \(g(y)\) — don't just add integrals; include \(+C\)
  • Green's: \(Q_x - P_y\), counterclockwise
  • Surface integrals: substitute \(z = g\), include \(\sqrt{g_x^2 + g_y^2 + 1}\)
  • Divergence theorem only on a closed surface
  • Shadow region: set \(z = 0\) to find where the surface meets the plane
  • Look for symmetry zeros (\(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta = 0\)) before grinding
