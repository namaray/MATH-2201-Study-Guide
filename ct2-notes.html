1Scalar Triple Product

The one formula behind volume and coplanarity.

$$ \mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} $$
Volume of parallelepiped \(= \big|\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})\big|\)  ·  Coplanar \(\iff \mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = 0\)

Why: coplanar vectors lie flat in one plane, so the "box" they make is squashed — it has zero volume.

⚠ Volume is always positive — take the modulus. A negative value only means the vectors are left-handed.

2Cross Product (the workhorse)

$$ \mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \end{vmatrix} = (a_2b_3 - a_3b_2)\mathbf{i} - (a_1b_3 - a_3b_1)\mathbf{j} + (a_1b_2 - a_2b_1)\mathbf{k} $$
⚠ The middle term carries a minus sign. This is the single most common CT mistake.

The result is perpendicular to both vectors — which is exactly why it gives you the normal of a plane.

3Equation of a Plane

Three ways to write the same plane
FormEquation
Vector (parametric)\(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + s\mathbf{u} + t\mathbf{v}\)
Vector (normal)\((\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{a})\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0\)  or  \(\mathbf{r}\cdot\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\)
Cartesian\(n_1x + n_2y + n_3z = d\),   where \(d = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\)
Read the normal straight off the Cartesian equation: \(2x - y + 2z = 5 \Rightarrow \mathbf{n} = \langle 2,-1,2\rangle\).
Case A — plane through three points \(A, B, C\)
  1. \(\overrightarrow{AB} = B - A\),   \(\overrightarrow{AC} = C - A\)
  2. \(\mathbf{n} = \overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}\)
  3. \(n_1(x-a_1) + n_2(y-a_2) + n_3(z-a_3) = 0\), then expand
Case B — plane through \(P\), parallel to \(\mathbf{u}\) and \(\mathbf{v}\)

The vector equation is immediate: \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{p} + s\mathbf{u} + t\mathbf{v}\). For the Cartesian form, take \(\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\) and continue as above.

Always check your answer by substituting the given points back in. It takes 10 seconds and catches every sign error.

4Line Meets Plane

Line \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + t\mathbf{d}\), plane \(n_1x+n_2y+n_3z = d\).

  1. Write the line in parametric form: \(x = a_1 + td_1\), \(y = a_2 + td_2\), \(z = a_3 + td_3\)
  2. Substitute all three into the plane equation
  3. Solve the resulting linear equation for \(t\)
  4. Put that \(t\) back into the line to get the point
For a horizontal plane \(z = c\) (aircraft-type questions), only the \(z\)-component matters — set \(a_3 + td_3 = c\) and solve.

Special cases: if \(\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0\) the line is parallel to the plane — either no solution (no intersection) or \(0=0\) (the line lies in the plane).

5Angles — Cosine or Sine?

Plane ↔ Plane  →  cosine
$$ \cos\theta = \frac{|\mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2}|}{|\mathbf{n_1}|\,|\mathbf{n_2}|} $$
Line ↔ Plane  →  sine
$$ \sin\theta = \frac{|\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{n}|}{|\mathbf{d}|\,|\mathbf{n}|} $$

How to remember: the normal sticks out of the plane, so the angle you compute with it is \(90^\circ - \theta\). Flipping cosine to sine does that flip for you automatically.

Line ↔ Line  →  cosine of the two directions
$$ \cos\theta = \frac{|\mathbf{d_1}\cdot\mathbf{d_2}|}{|\mathbf{d_1}|\,|\mathbf{d_2}|} $$
⚠ Keep the absolute value on top whenever the question asks for the acute angle. Without it you get the obtuse supplement (e.g. \(116.39^\circ\) instead of \(63.61^\circ\)).

6Perpendicular & Parallel Conditions

RelationshipPerpendicularParallel
Plane & plane\(\mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2} = 0\)\(\mathbf{n_1} = \lambda\mathbf{n_2}\)
Line & plane\(\mathbf{d} = \lambda\mathbf{n}\)\(\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0\)
Line & line\(\mathbf{d_1}\cdot\mathbf{d_2} = 0\)\(\mathbf{d_1} = \lambda\mathbf{d_2}\)
⚠ Note the swap on the middle row: a line ⟂ plane means the line is along the normal, and a line ∥ plane means it is ⟂ to the normal. This trips people up under time pressure.

Typical "find \(k\)" question: write both normals, set the dot product to \(0\), solve the linear equation in \(k\).

Last-minute checklist

  • Minus sign on the \(\mathbf{j}\) term of every cross product.
  • Modulus on volume, and on the dot product in every angle formula.
  • Sine for line–plane, cosine for plane–plane.
  • Subtract in the right order: \(\overrightarrow{AB} = B - A\), not \(A - B\).
  • The normal comes from the coefficients of \(x, y, z\) — ignore the constant.
  • Substitute the given points back into your plane equation to verify.
  • Give units where the question has them (m³ for a container, seconds for time).
  • You may simplify a normal by a common factor: \(\langle 2,2,4\rangle \to \langle 1,1,2\rangle\).
