Exercise on Conic Sections — Solutions

MATH-2201 Finals  ·  all four questions from the conics exercise sheet, fully worked

Questions

  1. Identify two conics and find centre, axes, vertices, foci, eccentricity
  2. Parabola — vertex, focus, directrix, axis, latus rectum
  3. Ellipse — centre, axes, vertices, co-vertices, foci, eccentricity
  4. Rotation of axes — eliminate the \(xy\) term

Q1. Identify the Conics Completing the Square

Identify the following conics: $$ 9x^2 + 16y^2 - 54x + 64y - 47 = 0 $$ $$ 16x^2 - 9y^2 - 64x - 54y - 161 = 0 $$ Find their centre, lengths of the major and minor axes, vertices, foci, and eccentricity.

(a) \(9x^2 + 16y^2 - 54x + 64y - 47 = 0\)

Identify: coefficients of \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) are \(9\) and \(16\) — same sign, different values → ellipse.

Complete the square
$$ 9(x^2 - 6x) + 16(y^2 + 4y) = 47 $$
$$ 9(x^2 - 6x + 9) + 16(y^2 + 4y + 4) $$
$$ = 47 + 9(9) + 16(4) $$
$$ 9(x-3)^2 + 16(y+2)^2 = 192 $$
$$ \frac{(x-3)^2}{64/3} + \frac{(y+2)^2}{12} = 1 $$
Read off the values

\(\tfrac{64}{3} \approx 21.3 > 12\), so \(a^2 = \tfrac{64}{3}\) sits under \(x\): the major axis is horizontal.

$$ a = \frac{8}{\sqrt3} = \frac{8\sqrt3}{3} \approx 4.619 $$
$$ b = \sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt3 \approx 3.464 $$
$$ c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{\tfrac{64}{3} - 12} $$
$$ = \sqrt{\tfrac{28}{3}} = \frac{2\sqrt{21}}{3} \approx 3.055 $$

TypeEllipse, horizontal major axis
Centre\((3, -2)\)
Major axis length \(2a\)\(\tfrac{16\sqrt3}{3}\)\(\approx 9.24\)
Minor axis length \(2b\)\(4\sqrt3\)\(\approx 6.93\)
Vertices\(\left(3 \pm \tfrac{8\sqrt3}{3},\, -2\right)\)\((7.62, -2),\ (-1.62, -2)\)
Foci\(\left(3 \pm \tfrac{2\sqrt{21}}{3},\, -2\right)\)\((6.06, -2),\ (-0.06, -2)\)

These numbers aren't round — possibly a typo on the sheet. If the constant were \(+1\) instead of \(-47\), the right-hand side would be 144, giving the tidy textbook ellipse \(\tfrac{(x-3)^2}{16} + \tfrac{(y+2)^2}{9} = 1\) with \(a = 4\), \(b = 3\), \(c = \sqrt7\), vertices \((7,-2), (-1,-2)\), foci \((3 \pm \sqrt7, -2)\). The eccentricity \(\sqrt7/4\) is the same either way. The solution above follows the sheet exactly as written — worth confirming the sign with your lecturer.

(b) \(16x^2 - 9y^2 - 64x - 54y - 161 = 0\)

Identify: coefficients \(16\) and \(-9\) have opposite signs → hyperbola.

Complete the square
$$ 16(x^2 - 4x) - 9(y^2 + 6y) = 161 $$
$$ 16(x^2 - 4x + 4) - 9(y^2 + 6y + 9) $$
$$ = 161 + 16(4) - 9(9) $$
$$ 16(x-2)^2 - 9(y+3)^2 = 144 $$
$$ \frac{(x-2)^2}{9} - \frac{(y+3)^2}{16} = 1 $$
Careful with the \(-9\): adding 9 inside \(-9(\;\cdot\;)\) adds \(-81\) to the left, so you add \(-81\) to the right too.
Read off the values

The positive term is under \(x\), so it opens left–right and \(a^2 = 9\), even though \(16 > 9\).

$$ a = 3 $$
$$ b = 4 $$
$$ c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5 $$
PropertyValue
TypeHyperbola, opens left–right
Centre\((2, -3)\)
Transverse axis length \(2a\)\(6\)
Conjugate axis length \(2b\)\(8\)
Vertices\((2 \pm 3, -3) = (5, -3),\ (-1, -3)\)
Foci\((2 \pm 5, -3) = (7, -3),\ (-3, -3)\)
Eccentricity\(e = \tfrac{5}{3} \approx 1.667\)
Asymptotes (bonus)\(y + 3 = \pm\tfrac43(x - 2)\)
For a hyperbola, "major and minor axes" means the transverse axis (\(2a\), through the vertices) and the conjugate axis (\(2b\)). Say so in your answer to show you know the difference.

Q2. Parabola Parabola

For the parabola \((x-2)^2 = 12(y+1)\), find the vertex, focus, directrix, axis of symmetry, and length of the latus rectum.
Match to the standard form
$$ (x-h)^2 = 4a(y-k) $$
$$ h = 2,\quad k = -1,\quad 4a = 12 \;\Rightarrow\; a = 3 $$

\(x\) is squared and \(a > 0\), so the parabola opens upward.

Read off everything
  • Vertex: \((h, k) = (2, -1)\)
  • Focus: \(a\) units above the vertex: \((h, k + a) = (2, 2)\)
  • Directrix: \(a\) units below the vertex: \(y = k - a = -4\)
  • Axis of symmetry: the vertical line through the vertex: \(x = 2\)
  • Latus rectum: \(|4a| = 12\), running from \((-4, 2)\) to \((8, 2)\)
Vertex \((2, -1)\) · Focus \((2, 2)\) · Directrix \(y = -4\) · Axis \(x = 2\) · Latus rectum \(= 12\)
Check: the vertex \(y = -1\) is exactly halfway between the focus \(y = 2\) and the directrix \(y = -4\). ✓ And the latus-rectum endpoint \((8, 2)\) is 6 from the focus and 6 from the directrix. ✓

Q3. Ellipse Ellipse

For the ellipse \(\dfrac{(x-3)^2}{25} + \dfrac{(y+2)^2}{9} = 1\), find the centre, major and minor axes, vertices, co-vertices, foci, and eccentricity.
Read off \(h, k, a, b\)

Already in standard form. The larger denominator, 25, is under \(x\) → major axis horizontal.

$$ h = 3,\qquad k = -2 $$
$$ a = 5,\quad b = 3,\quad c = \sqrt{25 - 9} = 4 $$
PropertyValue
1. Centre\((3, -2)\)
2. Major / minor axisMajor \(2a = 10\) (horizontal, along \(y = -2\)); minor \(2b = 6\) (vertical, along \(x = 3\))
3. Vertices\((3 \pm 5, -2) = (8, -2),\ (-2, -2)\)
4. Co-vertices\((3, -2 \pm 3) = (3, 1),\ (3, -5)\)
5. Foci\((3 \pm 4, -2) = (7, -2),\ (-1, -2)\)
6. Eccentricity\(e = \tfrac{c}{a} = \tfrac{4}{5} = 0.8\)
Check: foci \((7,-2)\) and \((-1,-2)\) lie between the vertices \((8,-2)\) and \((-2,-2)\), and \(e = 0.8 < 1\). ✓

Q4. Rotation of Axes Rotation

Consider the equation \(3x^2 + 4xy + 3y^2 - 10x + 2y - 5 = 0\).
  • Identify the type of conic.
  • Determine the angle through which the axes should be rotated to eliminate the \(xy\)-term.
  • Write the equation after rotation of axes.

Identify the conic

$$ A = 3,\quad B = 4,\quad C = 3 $$
$$ D = -10,\quad E = 2,\quad F = -5 $$
$$ B^2 - 4AC = 16 - 36 = -20 < 0 $$
Discriminant negative → the conic is an ellipse.

Angle of rotation

$$ \cot 2\theta = \frac{A - C}{B} = \frac{3 - 3}{4} = 0 $$
$$ \;\Longrightarrow\; 2\theta = 90^\circ \;\Longrightarrow\; \theta = 45^\circ $$
Rotate the axes through \(\theta = 45^\circ\) \(\left(= \tfrac{\pi}{4}\right)\).

Equation after rotation

With \(\cos 45^\circ = \sin 45^\circ = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\):

$$ x = \frac{x' - y'}{\sqrt2}, \qquad y = \frac{x' + y'}{\sqrt2} $$
Quadratic terms
$$ 3x^2 = \tfrac32\,(x'^2 - 2x'y' + y'^2) $$
$$ 4xy = 2\,(x'^2 - y'^2) $$
$$ 3y^2 = \tfrac32\,(x'^2 + 2x'y' + y'^2) $$

Adding: the \(x'y'\) terms cancel (that's the point of the rotation), leaving

$$ 3x'^2 + 3y'^2 + 2x'^2 - 2y'^2 = 5x'^2 + y'^2 $$
Linear terms
$$ -10x = -\tfrac{10}{\sqrt2}(x' - y') = -5\sqrt2\,x' + 5\sqrt2\,y' $$
$$ 2y = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt2}(x' + y') = \sqrt2\,x' + \sqrt2\,y' $$
$$ \text{Sum: } -4\sqrt2\,x' + 6\sqrt2\,y' $$
$$ 5x'^2 + y'^2 - 4\sqrt2\,x' + 6\sqrt2\,y' - 5 = 0 $$
Check with the invariant: \(A' + C' = 5 + 1 = 6 = A + C\). ✓ And the new discriminant \(0 - 4(5)(1) = -20\) matches the old one. ✓

Bonus — standard form (not asked, but it confirms the answer)

$$ 5\left(x'^2 - \tfrac{4\sqrt2}{5}x'\right) + \left(y'^2 + 6\sqrt2\,y'\right) = 5 $$
$$ 5\left(x' - \tfrac{2\sqrt2}{5}\right)^2 - \tfrac85 + \left(y' + 3\sqrt2\right)^2 - 18 = 5 $$
$$ 5\left(x' - \tfrac{2\sqrt2}{5}\right)^2 + \left(y' + 3\sqrt2\right)^2 = \tfrac{123}{5} $$
$$ \frac{\left(x' - \frac{2\sqrt2}{5}\right)^2}{123/25} + \frac{\left(y' + 3\sqrt2\right)^2}{123/5} = 1 $$

A genuine ellipse (the right-hand side is positive) with semi-axes \(\sqrt{123/5} \approx 4.96\) along \(y'\) and \(\sqrt{123}/5 \approx 2.22\) along \(x'\). Its centre is \(\left(\tfrac{2\sqrt2}{5}, -3\sqrt2\right)\) in the rotated frame, which is \(\left(\tfrac{17}{5}, -\tfrac{13}{5}\right) = (3.4, -2.6)\) in the original \(xy\)-frame.
