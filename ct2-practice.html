📐 Formula sheet for this CT
Scalar triple product
$$ \mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = \begin{vmatrix} a_1 & a_2 & a_3 \\ b_1 & b_2 & b_3 \\ c_1 & c_2 & c_3 \end{vmatrix} $$
  • Volume of parallelepiped \(= \big|\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})\big|\)
  • Three vectors are coplanar \(\iff \mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = 0\)
Planes
  • Normal from two in-plane vectors: \(\mathbf{n} = \mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}\)
  • Vector (parametric) form: \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{a} + s\mathbf{u} + t\mathbf{v}\)
  • Normal form: \((\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{a})\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0\)
  • Cartesian: \(n_1x + n_2y + n_3z = \mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{n}\)
Angles
$$ \cos\theta_{\text{plane–plane}} = \frac{|\mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2}|}{|\mathbf{n_1}||\mathbf{n_2}|}, \qquad \sin\theta_{\text{line–plane}} = \frac{|\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{n}|}{|\mathbf{d}||\mathbf{n}|} $$
  • Planes perpendicular \(\iff \mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2} = 0\); parallel \(\iff \mathbf{n_1} \parallel \mathbf{n_2}\)
  • Line–plane: use sine (not cosine) — the normal is perpendicular to the plane, so the angle you want is the complement.

Q1. Volume of a Parallelepiped Triple Product

Given \(\mathbf{a}\langle 1,2,3\rangle\), \(\mathbf{b}\langle 2,-1,1\rangle\) and \(C(3,1,2)\). Find the volume of the parallelepiped formed by these three vectors.
Solution

Volume \(= \big|\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})\big|\). First find \(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}\):

$$ \mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 2 & -1 & 1 \\ 3 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = \big[(-1)(2)-(1)(1)\big]\mathbf{i} - \big[(2)(2)-(1)(3)\big]\mathbf{j} + \big[(2)(1)-(-1)(3)\big]\mathbf{k} $$
$$ = -3\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + 5\mathbf{k} $$
$$ \mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = (1)(-3) + (2)(-1) + (3)(5) = -3 - 2 + 15 = 10 $$
Volume \(= |10| = 10\) cubic units
The absolute value matters: a negative triple product just means the three vectors form a left-handed set, but volume is never negative.

Q2. Coplanarity Triple Product

Determine whether the following three vectors are coplanar: \(\mathbf{a}\langle 1,2,-1\rangle\), \(\mathbf{b}\langle 2,-1,3\rangle\) and \(C(4,3,1)\).
Solution

Three vectors are coplanar if and only if their scalar triple product is zero.

$$ \mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \\ 4 & 3 & 1 \end{vmatrix} = \big[(-1)(1)-(3)(3)\big]\mathbf{i} - \big[(2)(1)-(3)(4)\big]\mathbf{j} + \big[(2)(3)-(-1)(4)\big]\mathbf{k} $$
$$ = -10\mathbf{i} + 10\mathbf{j} + 10\mathbf{k} $$
$$ \mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c}) = (1)(-10) + (2)(10) + (-1)(10) = -10 + 20 - 10 = 0 $$
The triple product is \(0\)  ⟹  the three vectors are coplanar (they enclose zero volume).

Q3. Volume of a Container Triple Product

A three-dimensional container is constructed using three displacement vectors: \(\mathbf{P}\langle 4,0,1\rangle\), \(\mathbf{Q}\langle 1,3,0\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{R}\langle 0,1,2\rangle\) in meters. Assuming the container forms a parallelepiped, find its volume.
Solution
$$ \mathbf{Q}\times\mathbf{R} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = \big[(3)(2)-(0)(1)\big]\mathbf{i} - \big[(1)(2)-(0)(0)\big]\mathbf{j} + \big[(1)(1)-(3)(0)\big]\mathbf{k} $$
$$ = 6\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k} $$
$$ \mathbf{P}\cdot(\mathbf{Q}\times\mathbf{R}) = (4)(6) + (0)(-2) + (1)(1) = 24 + 0 + 1 = 25 $$
Volume \(= 25 \text{ m}^3\)

Q4. Plane Through Three Points Equation of a Plane

Find the vector equation of the plane passing through the three points \(A(1,3,2)\), \(B(3,1,2)\) and \(C(2,4,1)\). Also find its Cartesian equation.
Step 1 — Two vectors lying in the plane
$$ \overrightarrow{AB} = B - A = \langle 2,-2,0\rangle, \qquad \overrightarrow{AC} = C - A = \langle 1,1,-1\rangle $$
Step 2 — Normal vector
$$ \mathbf{n} = \overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 2 & -2 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & -1 \end{vmatrix} = \big[(-2)(-1)-(0)(1)\big]\mathbf{i} - \big[(2)(-1)-(0)(1)\big]\mathbf{j} + \big[(2)(1)-(-2)(1)\big]\mathbf{k} $$
$$ \mathbf{n} = 2\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + 4\mathbf{k} \;\;\parallel\;\; \langle 1,1,2\rangle $$
Step 3 — Vector equation (parametric form)
$$ \mathbf{r} = \langle 1,3,2\rangle + s\langle 2,-2,0\rangle + t\langle 1,1,-1\rangle $$
Step 4 — Vector equation (normal form) and Cartesian equation
$$ (\mathbf{r} - \mathbf{a})\cdot\mathbf{n} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf{r}\cdot\langle 1,1,2\rangle = \langle 1,3,2\rangle\cdot\langle 1,1,2\rangle = 1 + 3 + 4 = 8 $$
$$ x + y + 2z = 8 $$
Vector equation: \(\mathbf{r} = \langle 1,3,2\rangle + s\langle 2,-2,0\rangle + t\langle 1,1,-1\rangle\),  or  \(\mathbf{r}\cdot\langle 1,1,2\rangle = 8\)
Cartesian equation: \(x + y + 2z = 8\)
Check all three points: \(A: 1+3+4=8\) ✓   \(B: 3+1+4=8\) ✓   \(C: 2+4+2=8\) ✓

Q5. Plane Through a Point, Parallel to Two Vectors Equation of a Plane

Find the vector equation of the plane passing through \(P(2,-1,3)\) and parallel to the vectors \(\mathbf{Q}\langle 1,2,-1\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{R}\langle 2,-1,3\rangle\).
Vector equation (parametric form)

The point gives the position vector; the two parallel vectors are the direction vectors.

$$ \mathbf{r} = \langle 2,-1,3\rangle + s\langle 1,2,-1\rangle + t\langle 2,-1,3\rangle $$
Normal form (and Cartesian equation)
$$ \mathbf{n} = \mathbf{Q}\times\mathbf{R} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 1 & 2 & -1 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 \end{vmatrix} = \big[(2)(3)-(-1)(-1)\big]\mathbf{i} - \big[(1)(3)-(-1)(2)\big]\mathbf{j} + \big[(1)(-1)-(2)(2)\big]\mathbf{k} $$
$$ = 5\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j} - 5\mathbf{k} \;\;\parallel\;\; \langle 1,-1,-1\rangle $$
$$ \mathbf{r}\cdot\langle 1,-1,-1\rangle = \langle 2,-1,3\rangle\cdot\langle 1,-1,-1\rangle = 2 + 1 - 3 = 0 $$
Vector equation: \(\mathbf{r} = \langle 2,-1,3\rangle + s\langle 1,2,-1\rangle + t\langle 2,-1,3\rangle\)
Cartesian equation: \(x - y - z = 0\)
The constant came out \(0\), which simply means this plane passes through the origin.

Q6. Line–Plane Intersection Line & Plane

The line \(\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j} + 3\mathbf{k} + t(2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j} + \mathbf{k})\) intersects the plane \(2x + y - z = 5\). Find the point of intersection.
Step 1 — Parametric coordinates of the line
$$ x = 1 + 2t, \qquad y = 2 - t, \qquad z = 3 + t $$
Step 2 — Substitute into the plane equation
$$ 2(1+2t) + (2-t) - (3+t) = 5 $$
$$ 2 + 4t + 2 - t - 3 - t = 5 \;\Longrightarrow\; 2t + 1 = 5 \;\Longrightarrow\; t = 2 $$
Step 3 — Put \(t = 2\) back into the line
$$ x = 1 + 4 = 5, \qquad y = 2 - 2 = 0, \qquad z = 3 + 2 = 5 $$
Point of intersection: \((5,\,0,\,5)\)
Check: \(2(5) + 0 - 5 = 5\) ✓

Q7. Aircraft Crossing a Horizontal Plane Line & Plane

An aircraft follows the path \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 100+20t,\; 50+10t,\; 500-25t\rangle\), where the coordinates are measured in meters and \(t\) is measured in seconds. The aircraft is required to cross the horizontal plane \(z = 200\).
(a) At what time does the aircraft reach the plane?
(b) Find the coordinates of the point where it crosses the plane.
(a) Time of crossing

The plane \(z = 200\) only constrains the \(z\)-component:

$$ 500 - 25t = 200 \;\Longrightarrow\; 25t = 300 \;\Longrightarrow\; t = 12 $$
The aircraft reaches the plane at \(t = 12\) seconds.
(b) Point of crossing
$$ x = 100 + 20(12) = 340, \qquad y = 50 + 10(12) = 170, \qquad z = 200 $$
Crossing point: \((340,\,170,\,200)\) meters

Q8. Solar Panel — Cartesian Equation of the Plane Equation of a Plane

A rectangular solar panel is mounted on a roof. Three points on the panel are \(A(2,1,4)\), \(B(6,2,5)\), \(C(3,5,6)\), where the coordinates are measured in meters. Find the Cartesian equation of the plane.
Step 1 — Two vectors in the plane
$$ \overrightarrow{AB} = \langle 4,1,1\rangle, \qquad \overrightarrow{AC} = \langle 1,4,2\rangle $$
Step 2 — Normal vector
$$ \mathbf{n} = \overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC} = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} & \mathbf{j} & \mathbf{k} \\ 4 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 4 & 2 \end{vmatrix} = \big[(1)(2)-(1)(4)\big]\mathbf{i} - \big[(4)(2)-(1)(1)\big]\mathbf{j} + \big[(4)(4)-(1)(1)\big]\mathbf{k} $$
$$ \mathbf{n} = -2\mathbf{i} - 7\mathbf{j} + 15\mathbf{k} $$
Step 3 — Plane through \(A\) with normal \(\mathbf{n}\)
$$ -2(x-2) - 7(y-1) + 15(z-4) = 0 $$
$$ -2x + 4 - 7y + 7 + 15z - 60 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; -2x - 7y + 15z - 49 = 0 $$
Cartesian equation: \(2x + 7y - 15z + 49 = 0\),  i.e.  \(-2x - 7y + 15z = 49\)
Check: \(B(6,2,5)\): \(-12-14+75 = 49\) ✓   \(C(3,5,6)\): \(-6-35+90 = 49\) ✓

Q9. Acute Angle Between Two Planes Angles

Find the acute angle between the planes \(2x - y + 2z = 5\) and \(x + 2y - 2z = 7\).
Solution

The angle between two planes equals the angle between their normals.

$$ \mathbf{n_1} = \langle 2,-1,2\rangle, \qquad \mathbf{n_2} = \langle 1,2,-2\rangle $$
$$ \mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2} = (2)(1) + (-1)(2) + (2)(-2) = 2 - 2 - 4 = -4 $$
$$ |\mathbf{n_1}| = \sqrt{4+1+4} = 3, \qquad |\mathbf{n_2}| = \sqrt{1+4+4} = 3 $$
$$ \cos\theta = \frac{|\mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2}|}{|\mathbf{n_1}||\mathbf{n_2}|} = \frac{|-4|}{(3)(3)} = \frac{4}{9} \approx 0.4444 $$
\(\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.4444) \approx 63.61^\circ\)
Take the absolute value of the dot product — without it you get \(116.39^\circ\), the obtuse supplement, and the question asks for the acute angle.

Q10. Value of \(k\) for Perpendicular Planes Angles

Find the value of \(k\) such that the planes \(x + 2y + kz = 4\) and \(2x - y + 2z = 5\) are perpendicular.
Solution

Two planes are perpendicular exactly when their normals are perpendicular, i.e. \(\mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2} = 0\).

$$ \mathbf{n_1} = \langle 1,2,k\rangle, \qquad \mathbf{n_2} = \langle 2,-1,2\rangle $$
$$ \mathbf{n_1}\cdot\mathbf{n_2} = (1)(2) + (2)(-1) + (k)(2) = 2 - 2 + 2k = 2k $$
$$ 2k = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; k = 0 $$
\(k = 0\)
So the first plane is \(x + 2y = 4\) — a vertical plane, which is indeed perpendicular to \(2x - y + 2z = 5\).

Q11. Angle Between a Line and a Plane Angles

Find the angle between the line \(\mathbf{r} = \langle 2,3,-1\rangle + t\langle 3,-1,2\rangle\) and the plane \(x + 3y - 2z = 5\).
Solution

Use the sine formula, because the normal is perpendicular to the plane:

$$ \sin\theta = \frac{|\mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{n}|}{|\mathbf{d}|\,|\mathbf{n}|} $$
$$ \mathbf{d} = \langle 3,-1,2\rangle \;(\text{direction of line}), \qquad \mathbf{n} = \langle 1,3,-2\rangle \;(\text{normal of plane}) $$
$$ \mathbf{d}\cdot\mathbf{n} = (3)(1) + (-1)(3) + (2)(-2) = 3 - 3 - 4 = -4 $$
$$ |\mathbf{d}| = \sqrt{9+1+4} = \sqrt{14}, \qquad |\mathbf{n}| = \sqrt{1+9+4} = \sqrt{14} $$
$$ \sin\theta = \frac{|-4|}{\sqrt{14}\cdot\sqrt{14}} = \frac{4}{14} = \frac{2}{7} \approx 0.2857 $$
\(\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.2857) \approx 16.60^\circ\)
If you had used \(\cos^{-1}(2/7) \approx 73.40^\circ\) you would have found the angle between the line and the normal — the complement of the answer.
